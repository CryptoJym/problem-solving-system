## Executive Summary

The public Problem Solving System is already strong where many problem-solving
systems are weak: it improves the front end of messy work before anyone commits
to a favorite fix, and it treats solutions as hypotheses until they are tested.
That leverage-first architecture should stay.

The highest-confidence refinement is not a broad expansion. The strict 80/20
move is to add one new core skill between prototyping and operating rhythm:

`Stakeholder Alignment and Adoption Design`

The reason is that the current implementation skill starts with cadence,
ownership, communication, training, and review. Those are useful, but adjacent
disciplines show that real-world adoption also depends on context fit, local
barriers and facilitators, acceptability, feasibility, fidelity, champions, and
psychological safety for surfacing problems early.

The report recommends:

- Keep the leverage-first architecture.
- Add one new skill: stakeholder alignment and adoption design.
- Reorder the learning path so structured ideation comes before decision
  narrowing.
- Treat measurement as a cross-cutting thread, not a standalone new skill.
- Defer niche additions until usage data shows recurring failure modes.

## Skill-By-Skill Refinement Table

| Skill | What to keep | Highest-value refinement | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PS-01 Problem Framing and Reframing | Keep the move that strips hidden fixes from complaints. | Add a mandatory aim statement, system boundary, baseline, and guardrail/balancing measure. | Refine inside existing skill |
| PS-02 Observation and Problem Finding | Keep direct observation as a separate domain. | Upgrade generic observation into contextual inquiry: observe in the real environment, validate recall with observation, record external resources, interruptions, workarounds, and edge cases, and separate what users say from what they do. | Refine inside existing skill |
| PS-03 Root Cause and Constraint Mapping | Keep cause mapping plus constraint focus. | Avoid the single-root-cause trap, identify the active constraint, and rank remedies by action strength so the team does not stop at weak fixes like training or policy memos alone. | Refine inside existing skill |
| PS-04 Decision Hygiene and Option Widening | Keep the anti-tunnel rule. | Add one outside-view/base-rate check, one premortem, and explicit kill criteria/tripwires. In the final learning path, place this after ideation so the stack diverges before it converges. | Refine inside existing skill |
| PS-05 Reality Testing and Evidence Seeking | Keep zoom-out/zoom-in evidence and disagreement seeking. | Add an evidence register that distinguishes research evidence, local data, stakeholder evidence, and practitioner expertise, plus a lightweight critical-appraisal check and confidence rating for each must-be-true claim. | Refine inside existing skill |
| PS-06 Structured Ideation and Brainwriting | Keep it as a distinct skill. | Preserve silent first-round writing and add a deliberate combination/analogy round before voting. | Keep as-is |
| PS-07 Rapid Prototyping and Experiments | Keep one-assumption-at-a-time testing and low-fidelity prototypes. | Add a standard experiment card: focal assumption, smallest test, prediction, success/failure threshold, data to collect, and next decision. ## Recommended Final Skill Stack

1. Problem Framing and Reframing
2. Observation and Problem Finding
3. Root Cause and Constraint Mapping
4. Structured Ideation and Brainwriting
5. Decision Hygiene and Option Selection
6. Reality Testing and Evidence Seeking
7. Rapid Prototyping and Experiments
8. Stakeholder Alignment and Adoption Design
9. Operating Rhythm and Sustainment
10. Learning Mindset and After-Action Review

The main sequencing change is to move ideation before decision hygiene in the
published learning path. Framing, observation, and cause mapping remain the
front-end filter. The framework then diverges by generating options, converges
with decision hygiene, reality-tests and prototypes, then moves into adoption,
sustainment, and learning.

## Validation Tests

- Frame quality test: audit the first 20 real PS-01/PS-02 uses and measure how
  often the original complaint changes materially after observation.
- Option quality test: require artifacts showing at least three materially
  different options, one outside-view check, one premortem, and objective
  tripwires.
- Experiment quality test: audit whether each experiment shows one focal
  assumption, a prediction, a smallest-possible test, a measurement plan, and a
  next-decision rule.
- Adoption readiness test: after adding the new skill, compare implementation
  stall rate before and after. Measure acceptability, adoption, feasibility,
  fidelity, and sustainment at 30, 60, and 90 days.
- Learning-loop closure test: check whether each retrospective produces a named
  system update, owner, and review date, then track recurrence of the same
  failure class.
