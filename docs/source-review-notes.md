## Extracted Model

The course material describes a cycle:

1. Monitor, observe, and notice.
2. Frame, reframe, and analyze for insight.
3. Imagine and design solutions.
4. Prototype, test, and experiment to learn.

The project material shows the cycle in practice:

- The team collected many candidate problems before selecting one.
- The team narrowed candidates by feasibility, value, and fit for the project time box.
- The team used 5 Whys, CRT, and Theory of Constraints to formulate the warehouse productivity problem.
- Initial thinking favored stricter policy control, but the team reframed the issue as a lack of daily direction, structure, and communication.
- The tested solution was a daily huddle with goals, assignments, company context, open forum, and safety notes.
- Early observations suggested better coordination, more peer help, and stronger engagement.

## Reusable Principles

- Start with symptoms, not causes or solutions.
- Use ground rules to protect the quality of group thinking.
- Make people write independently before group discussion.
- Rank candidate problems by value and feasibility.
- Treat formulation as a first-class deliverable.
- Use multiple cause tools because each exposes different blind spots.
- Ask experts for base rates and implementation patterns, not just opinions.
- Prefer structured brainwriting over casual brainstorming.
- Prototype at the lowest fidelity that can teach the next thing.
- Convert solutions into simple daily operating rhythms.
- Treat unexpected results as data.
