# Rapid Prototyping and Experiments

## Use When

- The team has a promising option but uncertainty remains.
- Stakeholders are arguing abstractly.
- A behavior, workflow, policy, product, or communication change needs testing.
- Building the full solution would be expensive or slow.
- The team needs a written experiment card before acting.

## Inputs

- A solution concept or finalist.
- The riskiest assumption to test.
- A user, operator, stakeholder, or system surface where the test can run.
- A time box and acceptable risk level.
- A success, failure, or learning threshold.
- The decision that will be made after the test.

## Quick Start

Use a same-day prototype pass:

1. Choose one assumption, not the whole solution.
2. Choose the experiment type: MVP for product/customer demand or PDSA for workflow/process change.
3. Pick the lowest-fidelity artifact that can test it.
4. Write the prediction and threshold before testing.
5. Run the test with real or realistic users/systems.
6. Decide whether to discard, revise, retest, raise fidelity, adopt, or implement.

## Procedure

1. Identify the riskiest assumption.
2. Choose the experiment style:
   - MVP-style test for demand, usability, pricing, conversion, or product behavior
   - PDSA-style cycle for process, workflow, handoff, checklist, policy, or operating change
3. Choose the lowest-fidelity prototype that can test it:
   - sketch
   - wireframe
   - storyboard
   - role play
   - checklist
   - process map
   - small live pilot
   - theoretical model
4. Write an experiment card:
   - focal assumption
   - smallest test
   - prediction
   - success/failure threshold
   - data to collect
   - next decision rule
5. Run the test quickly.
6. Capture observations, not just opinions.
7. Decide whether to:
   - discard
   - revise
   - test again
   - increase fidelity
   - adopt into a limited rollout
   - implement

## Output

- Assumption tested
- Prototype artifact
- Prediction and threshold
- Observed result
- Data collected
- Learning
- Next decision

## Evidence Check

Before leaving this skill, confirm:

- The prototype tested a named assumption.
- The prediction, threshold, and decision rule were written before feedback came in.
- The experiment style fits the context: MVP for product/customer uncertainty, PDSA for workflow/process uncertainty.
- Observations are separated from opinions.
- The next decision follows from the evidence, not sunk cost.

## Handoff Prompt

```text
We are using Skill 7, Rapid Prototyping and Experiments. Concept: [concept]. Experiment style: [MVP/PDSA]. Assumption tested: [assumption]. Prototype: [artifact]. Prediction and threshold: [prediction]. Actual result: [summary]. Decision: [discard/revise/retest/increase fidelity/adopt/implement]. Please review whether the evidence supports the decision.
```

## Watchouts

- Do not over-prototype before the learning goal is clear.
- Do not validate only with friendly observers.
- Do not hide failed prototypes; they are learning assets.
- Do not run a pilot without a written threshold for what would change the next decision.
