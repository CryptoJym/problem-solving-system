# Stakeholder Alignment and Adoption Design

## Use When

- A prototype or evidence pass supports the idea, but uptake is uncertain.
- Success depends on multiple teams, incentives, permissions, tools, or local workflows.
- The solution is technically sound but may stall in the real organization.
- People need psychological safety to report problems early.
- A rollout would fail if champions, barriers, feasibility, or fidelity are ignored.

## Inputs

- The solution concept, experiment result, and decision rationale.
- Stakeholder groups affected by the change.
- The behavior or workflow that must change.
- Known barriers, facilitators, incentives, policies, and tool constraints.
- A candidate champion, sponsor, or operating owner.
- Adoption measures that can be checked at 30, 60, and 90 days.

## Quick Start

Use a 30-minute adoption pass before rollout:

1. Name the exact behavior or workflow that must change.
2. Map the few stakeholders who can block, enable, or normalize the change.
3. Identify the 20 percent of barriers most likely to stop adoption.
4. Design supports: champion, job aid, training, feedback path, and escalation path.
5. Define adoption outcomes before moving to operating rhythm.

## Procedure

1. State the change in behavioral terms:
   - who does what
   - when they do it
   - what artifact or signal proves it happened
2. Map stakeholder groups:
   - sponsor or decision owner
   - operating owner
   - local champions
   - primary users or operators
   - adjacent teams affected by the change
   - likely resisters or skeptics
3. Diagnose context fit:
   - workflow fit
   - incentive fit
   - tool and access fit
   - policy or compliance fit
   - time, staffing, and capacity fit
   - cultural or psychological safety risks
4. Identify barriers and facilitators.
5. Use the 80/20 rule: solve the few adoption barriers most likely to block rollout instead of designing for every edge case.
6. Design adoption supports:
   - named champion
   - plain communication script
   - job aid or checklist
   - training or practice moment
   - feedback channel
   - escalation path for blocked users
7. Define adoption outcomes:
   - acceptability: do users find it reasonable?
   - adoption: are people actually using it?
   - appropriateness: does it fit the work?
   - feasibility: can people do it with current resources?
   - fidelity: are people doing the intended behavior, not a distorted version?
   - cost: what time, money, or attention does it consume?
   - penetration: how much of the intended group is reached?
   - sustainment: does it keep happening after launch attention fades?
8. Make a readiness decision:
   - pilot in one context
   - adjust the solution or supports
   - delay rollout until a blocker is removed
   - hand off to Skill 8 for operating rhythm and sustainment

## Evidence Check

Before leaving this skill, confirm:

- The plan names the exact behavior that must change.
- The most important blockers and enablers were identified with local evidence, not assumptions alone.
- A champion or operating owner exists for the rollout context.
- Users have a safe path to report friction, confusion, or failure early.
- Adoption outcomes include both uptake and fidelity.
- The readiness decision explains whether to pilot, adjust, delay, or hand off to sustainment.

## Watchouts

- Do not mistake agreement in a meeting for adoption in the workflow.
- Do not rely on communication alone when tools, incentives, permissions, or capacity are the real blockers.
- Do not punish early reports of friction; they are implementation data.
- Do not hand off to sustainment until the rollout has an owner, champion, feedback path, and fidelity check.
