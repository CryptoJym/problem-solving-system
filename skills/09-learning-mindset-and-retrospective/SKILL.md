# Learning Mindset and After-Action Review

## Use When

- A prototype failed or produced unexpected results.
- A solution worked but the team does not know why.
- People are avoiding hard feedback.
- The system needs durable learning capture.
- The same failure class may recur unless the system changes.

## Inputs

- The original expectation, decision, or hypothesis.
- The actual result.
- Evidence from the test, launch, or implementation cycle.
- The people closest to the work.
- A place to store the reusable lesson.
- The owner who can change the relevant checklist, process, playbook, or rhythm.

## Quick Start

Use a 20-minute learning capture:

1. State expected vs actual without blame.
2. Separate facts, interpretations, and feelings.
3. Ask what helped, what hurt, and why.
4. Identify what was proved, disproved, or left unknown.
5. Convert the lesson into a reusable rule or checklist update with an owner.
6. Pick the next experiment, implementation adjustment, or stop decision.

## Procedure

1. State the expected result.
2. State the actual result.
3. Treat unexpected outcomes as data.
4. Keep the tone blame-light so people can name weak signals, mistakes, and uncomfortable facts.
5. Ask:
   - What was expected?
   - What actually happened?
   - What helped?
   - What hurt?
   - Why was there a gap?
   - What did this prove?
   - What did this disprove?
   - What did we learn about the system?
   - What should be repeated?
   - What should be stopped?
   - What should be tried next?
6. Capture corrective actions with owner and review date.
7. Update the skill, checklist, decision rule, or operating rhythm.

```text
Expectation:

Actual result:

Evidence:

```

## Output

- Expected vs actual result
- Failure or learning notes
- Updated assumptions
- Reusable rule
- Corrective action owner
- System update with review date
- Next experiment or implementation change

## Evidence Check

Before leaving this skill, confirm:

- The retrospective names evidence, not only feelings.
- At least one assumption was updated.
- The lesson is reusable outside the specific incident.
- A named owner can make the system update.
- The review date checks whether the update prevented recurrence.
- The next move is clear: repeat, stop, revise, or escalate.

## Handoff Prompt

```text
We are using Skill 9, Learning Mindset and After-Action Review. Expectation: [expected]. Actual: [actual]. Evidence: [evidence]. What helped/hurt: [summary]. Reusable lesson: [lesson]. System update: [update]. Owner and review date: [owner/date]. Next move: [move]. Please check whether this learning should update any earlier skill or operating rhythm.
```

## Watchouts

- Do not make failure personal.
- Do not call a test failed if it taught the needed lesson.
- Do not let success go unexamined.
- Do not let a retrospective end with insight but no system update.
