# Operating Rhythm and Sustainment

## Use When

- The chosen solution depends on people doing different work repeatedly.
- The problem involves unclear expectations, coordination, accountability, or feedback.
- A lightweight process may solve more than a heavy policy.
- Adoption barriers have been addressed, but the new behavior still needs cadence and ownership.

## Inputs

- The chosen solution and decision rationale.
- Adoption readiness notes from Skill 10, if rollout depends on people or teams.
- The repeated behavior required.
- Owners, participants, and affected stakeholders.
- A lightweight metric or observable signal.
- Communication and training constraints.

## Quick Start

Use a first-cycle implementation pass:

1. Translate the solution into a recurring behavior.
2. Assign one owner and one review cadence.
3. Define the visible signal of success.
4. Convert the behavior into standard work.
5. Review after the first cycle and adjust.

## Procedure

1. Translate the solution into a recurring cadence.
2. Confirm adoption design has already covered local fit, barriers, champions, and psychological safety when the change depends on people.
3. Keep the first rhythm simple:
   - daily or weekly huddle
   - top 1-3 goals
   - assignments
   - blockers
   - visible progress
   - open feedback
4. Convert the behavior into standard work:
   - trigger
   - owner
   - action
   - artifact
   - review point
5. Make success observable.
6. Train on expectations and job aids.
7. Add accountability only after expectations are clear.
8. Use incentives only for specific behaviors, not vague outcomes.
9. Review and adjust after the first cycle.

## Output

- Cadence
- Roles
- Metrics
- Communication script
- Training notes
- Standard work definition
- Feedback and escalation channel
- First review date

## Evidence Check

Before leaving this skill, confirm:

- The implementation is a behavior and cadence, not just an announcement.
- The behavior has a trigger, owner, artifact, and review point.
- Every recurring action has an owner.
- Success can be observed without a long debate.
- The first review date is scheduled before enforcement expands.
- Open feedback has a place to go during the first cycle.


## Watchouts

- Do not use policy as a substitute for leadership clarity.
- Do not incentivize what you cannot measure cleanly.
- Do not overload the first implementation with too many behaviors.
- Do not use this skill to skip adoption design when the real blocker is buy-in, context fit, or readiness.
